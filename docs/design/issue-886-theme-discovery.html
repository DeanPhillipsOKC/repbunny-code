Getting people to the themes

Chosen: S1 for setup (#886) and N2 for announcing a new theme (#887). Owner, 2026-10-31.

Two separate choices. Pick one option from each, or "neither". Every phone works: tap the theme links, Try it, and the theme tiles. Tapping a Premium theme previews it, the way free users can try themes today, with a Keep it with Premium bar.

1. In setup, how do themes show up?

Both build on option A from the look picker. Halloween is the "new" seasonal theme in these mockups.

Option S1 My pick

A link row under the two tiles

Pick your look

Cute or all business. It's your call.

Quiet, and still easy to find. The choice is still two tiles, so it stays simple. The colored dots hint that there's more without asking for a decision. Anyone curious taps it, and everyone else just hits Continue.

Option S2

A third tile: "More looks"

Pick your look

Cute or all business. It's your call.

The most visible. Themes get a tile as big as the main choice. That costs a few things: the first two tiles shrink, the step stops being a plain yes or no, and a Premium label shows up before anyone has logged a set. That's the closest of these four to a nag.

2. When a release brings a new theme, how does anyone hear about it?

Today, a release with a seasonal theme ships that season's icon as the app's default (decision #857), and the one-time "RepBunny just updated" banner shows on Home. Nothing points at the theme itself. Every option here shows once per release, and a single dismissal ends it.

Option N1

Pop-up at launch

New for October: Halloween

A new home-screen icon came with this update, plus a matching theme with candy-corn confetti.

Impossible to miss, and it gets in the way. It covers Start workout right when someone opens the app to train, and it shows to everyone on the update, including people who turned off update notes. It's the format people learn to dismiss without reading.

Option N2 My pick

Today's update banner, with Try it

Uses what's already there. It's the same one-time banner, in the same spot. When the release has a theme, the banner's first line talks about it and picks up a Try it button. Start workout stays reachable, and the existing "turn off update notes" setting covers it.

The best moment, and the busiest screen. They just finished, they feel good, and nothing is waiting on them. But the recap already carries the trial pick, the rating ask, the auto-copy prompt, and milestones, so this card would compete with those. It would need a rule like "only when nothing else is on the recap".

My picks are S1 and N2. Both make the themes findable without ever asking a new user for anything before their first workout. S1 is one quiet row in setup. N2 reuses a banner people already know how to close, and adds nothing new to turn off.

If you want a stronger push, N3 is the one I'd add next. It reaches people right after a workout, and I'd show it only when the recap has nothing else on it.
