# Decisions already made

The owner has ruled on these. **Read this page before you pitch, file, or build anything**, and don't re-propose a ruled-out idea. Only the owner can reopen one, and only by saying so (cite this page and the issue when you ask). Each entry gives the rule, why, and where the detail lives. Add a new entry here the day a decision is made, not only in a chat or a local memory file (#533).

Reviews (`pm-review`, `ux-review`, `security-audit`, `accessibility-review`), `refine-backlog`, `triage-support`, and `next-item` all read this page first.

## Product

- **Nothing the user logs leaves the phone. No exceptions, opt-in or not** (2026-10-09). No AI parsing of import files (#208 rejected), no analytics, no cloud features. Crash reporting stays on the device with a one-tap "Send a report?" (#234) and the store dashboards (#235). Full rule: `docs/architecture.md` Operational notes. Our own marketing text going to a voice or image tool is fine; user data is not.
- **No embedding models or vector search in the app** (2026-10-20, #693 and #694 closed). A cloud embedding API would send user text off the phone, and an on-device model adds 25 to 35 MB to a roughly 15 MB app. The owner judged neither worth it for import matching or guide search. Improve those with plain ranking, synonyms, and fuzzy matching instead. Don't re-propose on-device RAG or semantic search for the app.
- **The native app makes no network requests of its own to third-party hosts, and none to our own site without a privacy check** (2026-10-15). Any request tells the host the user's IP address and when they opened the app. Tapping a link that opens repbunny.app in the browser (guides, privacy, support) is fine because the user chose it. Don't add silently fetched images, fonts, scripts, or video. Bundle seasonal art small (inline SVG or compressed WebP); no media hosts like Imgur. If the bundle outgrows that, download-on-demand from our own site needs the owner's OK first. Don't bundle videos; link to the site instead.
- **No schedules, plans, or calendars** (2026-10-09). Templates are called **Routines** in user-facing text (#206). "Plans" was rejected because it implies a day-by-day calendar. Don't pitch weekly schedules or a "today's workout" assignment. Starter programs are a set of editable routines. A routine is what you repeat; a workout is one logged session. Why: people miss gym days, and a schedule makes it harder to pick a workout.
- **Milestones, not currency** (2026-10-05). No spendable balance, no shop, no prices, never streaks, no guilt. Rewards must not need new illustrated art or undercut Premium. A dress-up bunny needs professional artists and is shelved (#147 closed). See `docs/architecture/milestones.md`.
- **Extras are Premium-only and earned by training** (2026-10-07, #190). Free users earn only 7-day trial passes from milestones, and a trial pass doesn't unlock Extras. Don't re-propose "Premium gets everything up front" (#185) or free Extras.
- **Analytics split** (2026-10-05, #132). Free keeps the basic "am I getting stronger" signal: Lift progress list, Heaviest at 1M/3M, Muscle groups, Next time, PR celebrations. Premium gets Est. 1RM, Volume, 1Y/All ranges, the rep-max table, and new analytics by default. Locked items preview in the themes style with "Keep it with Premium", no bare padlocks.
- **Adaptive progression is allowed, but it must feel serious** (reopened 2026-10-05, #140, #160). Credible and evidence-based: e1RM or RPE-driven, double progression with deloads, transparent reasoning. Never a naive "+5 lb every time". Built only from the lifter's own sets, never filled in without a tap, switchable per exercise.
- **Premium has light tamper resistance only** (2026-10-03). Build-time flag, StoreKit 2 verified entitlements as the source of truth, fail open offline or on errors. No server receipt validation, obfuscation, jailbreak or root detection, and nothing that adds friction for real users to stop crackers.
- **Free Premium goes through the stores, never our own codes** (2026-10-12, #343, #344). Giveaways use Play promo codes and Apple offer codes. QA accounts are Play license testers. App Store guideline 3.1.1 bans in-app unlock codes, and a code server would be the app's first server contact. The dev toggle (#113) is compiled out of store builds; the personal Android build has it (#784).
- **Accessibility means usable, not redesigned for large type** (2026-10-15, #575). The app looks its best at default text size; don't pitch larger fonts or looser layouts for everyone. People who need bigger text use the phone's text-size setting, and at those sizes the app must stay usable (no overlap, clipping, or elements blending together, scrolling works, every control reachable), not pretty. A fix that makes the default look worse for most people is out: change behavior only at large sizes, or leave it. `/accessibility-review` files under this rule.
- **Ask for a store rating rarely, after a finished record workout** (2026-10-22, #776). Only on the finish recap, never mid-workout or during rest, and only when the workout set a record, at least 7 days after the first workout and 5 workouts in. At least 30 days between asks, at most 3 a year. Only the OS rating sheet, with no question or copy of our own before it (store rules forbid both). A Preferences toggle turns it off for good. Don't ask on install or in the first days, and don't pitch a custom "Enjoying RepBunny?" prompt.
- **The automatic copy is a launch blocker, and it's on by default** (2026-10-27, #432, #874). Native data lives only on the phone, so losing it is the catastrophic failure the privacy promise makes possible. The phone's own backups (Auto Backup, iCloud Backup) depend on the person's settings, run rarely, and restore only at phone setup, so they aren't enough on their own. A copy that depends on a decision is one most people won't have, so it's almost invisible: on by default in a folder the app picks (the app's iCloud Drive folder on iPhone, Documents/RepBunny on Android), with no question after a workout. Setup shows it once as information, not a choice. Turning it off is possible behind an honest warning, and Your data turns it back on. A folder the person picks takes priority. Restoring uses an in-app list of copies (Your data › Your copies, and the setup welcome on a new install), not a file browser. The copy goes to the person's own storage, never to us; Google Drive through its API is out (OAuth and a network call).
- **Seasonal app icons change with a store release, never inside the app by date** (2026-10-26, #857). iOS shows a "You have changed the icon" alert on every in-app switch, and some Android launchers drop the home-screen shortcut when the launcher alias changes. Cut release's `default_icon` ships the season's icon as the build's default instead, which is silent on both. In-app picks stay the user's own. Don't pitch a date-based in-app switch.
- **The bunny stays on by default; setup shows every theme as a free try-on** (2026-10-31, #886, #887; changed 2026-11-05, #1002). Setup step 2 has a Bunny art switch (on by default), Classic and Chalk as tiles (Classic selected), and every other theme as the same tiles under a closed More themes accordion. Setup never opens the theme sheet. Outfits, icons, and other combinations live in Preferences › Theme. The owner's reasoning for the change: nobody buys before their first workout, but people should know the other themes exist. So setup still sells nothing: no per-tile Premium tag and no price, only one line saying a pick is free for 7 days. Finishing setup on a Premium theme starts a one-time 7-day Themes trial (the #155 trial; it doesn't use a milestone pass). When it ends, the app goes back to Classic and the usual trial-end banner on Home offers Premium or Change theme, once. A release's new theme is announced only on the existing one-time update banner, with a Try it button (#887). There is no pop-up at launch or at trial end. A card on the finish recap is parked, not ruled out.
- **Animated exercise demos are parked until the app makes money** (2026-11-02, #901). The spike (`docs/design/issue-901-exercise-demos.md`) found free motion capture covers only about 3 of 355 exercises, and paid motion data or custom animation runs hundreds of dollars. The owner didn't like the results either way: the line figure, and a bunny redrawn in side view to match the mascot art. Parked, not ruled out: it's an ambitious feature for if RepBunny earns enough to pay for professional animation. Until then, don't pitch, file, or build exercise demo animations, including do-it-yourself capture with MediaPipe.
- **No Apple Watch support.** Entering weights and reps on a watch conflicts with fast set entry.
- **Name: just "RepBunny"** (2026-09-30). Don't use "Pocket Edition". A cloud-native version, if it ever exists, would be "RepBunny Cloud". The bunny branding stays (the audience is broader than hardcore lifters); renaming over GymBunny was declined. The one exception is the store listing title, which carries a keyword suffix for search ("RepBunny: Gym Workout Log" on Play, "RepBunny: Workout Tracker" on the App Store; 2026-10-21). The home screen and the app itself stay plain "RepBunny". See `docs/brand-identity.md` Naming and `docs/store-listing.md`.
- **Android-first promotion is on** (2026-10-30). Play approved the production app, so the owner is promoting the Android app now (intro videos for Facebook and TikTok, `scripts/demo-video/social-intro.mjs`), with the iPhone shown as "coming soon". On 2026-11-02, with the Play listing public, the owner also started the monthly marketing routine (`/marketing-review`, #577) ahead of the App Store launch: it files small `human` tasks and never posts anything itself. On 2026-11-06 the owner removed its limit of 3 tasks and 3 audit findings a month because they need to promote: it files everything worth doing, and a second run in a month files anything new. The rest of the hold below (agents pitching or filing marketing outside that routine) still waits for the App Store launch.
- **Marketing is on hold until the iPhone app is in the App Store** (2026-09-30). Don't propose or file social posts, promotion, or marketing automation; steer toward features and the launch path. The site stays live for the privacy and support URLs the stores need. The owner approved one exception on 2026-10-15: a monthly marketing routine that files `human` tasks for the owner (ads, social, follower growth). It was moved up to the Play launch on 2026-11-02 (see the entry above); outside that routine, nothing marketing-related is filed until the App Store launch.
- **No site analytics, not even Cloudflare's server-side numbers** (2026-10-15). The privacy page says repbunny.app has no analytics and that Cloudflare's logs exist only to deliver and protect the site. Reading them to measure marketing would stretch that promise, and the owner would rather not collect than dilute it. Marketing measures with store aggregates and a hand-kept costs sheet only (#584).
- **Accessibility means usable, not redesigned for large type** (2026-10-15). The app is designed to look its best at default text size. People who need bigger text use the phone's own text-size setting, and the app must stay readable and usable then: no overlapping, clipped, or blended-together elements, working scroll, and every control reachable. It does not have to look polished at the largest sizes, and the design never gets bigger fonts or looser layouts for everyone to make that case work. Also required: sufficient contrast, tap targets of at least 44 pt, and screen-reader labels. The monthly accessibility review (#575) judges against this.
- **PWA retired** (2026-11-04, #937). Both pilot users are on the native apps, so the PWA went first, ahead of making the code public: sign-in and its backend mode left the app (#938), then the PWA shell (#939), then the backend's scripts, SQL, test secrets, and docs (#940). The owner backs up the pilot data and shuts the hosting and backend down (#941). The app is the Android and iPhone builds only, with all data on the device. Don't propose bringing back a web app, accounts, or a backend.
- **Keep the personal Android flavor.** Don't propose removing or disabling the personal flavor or `release-android.yml` (the owner chose to keep it, 2026-10-09).

## How agents work here

- **Claude Code only** (2026-10-15, #586). No Codex skill mirrors, no `AGENTS.md`, no `.codex/` config. Skills live only in `.claude/skills/`. <!-- docs-refs: ignore -->
- **PWA parity: retired** (2026-11-04, #940). From 2026-10-06 every feature had to work in the PWA as well as the native app. The PWA is gone (see PWA retired above), so features are built and tested for the native apps only, through the local e2e specs. A schema change is a new SQLite migration (`src/data/sqlite/schema.ts`), with no human SQL step.
- **Governed delivery replaces direct to main** (2026-10-20, #688). The owner reversed the old "direct to main is deliberate" rule: the process should hold up to scrutiny as if the app handled sensitive data. Target state: every change, the owner's included, goes through a PR with no bypass (a written break-glass instead); independent CI is the gate, not the agent's own run; three risk tiers with an AI review on every PR and owner approval only on tier 3 (guardrails, data and schema, auth, permissions, native config); agents act as their own GitHub App identities. The repo stays private on GitHub Pro. No self-hosted e2e VM (changed 2026-10-21, #676 closed): agents still run e2e before merging, and a clean full e2e run on a GitHub-hosted runner gates every store release, so Cut release won't tag a build until it passes (#677). This is a budget trade-off and is written up as an accepted risk in `docs/governance/threat-model.md`. Revisit once the app makes enough money to pay for a runner, or if a regression gets past an agent's own run; otherwise don't re-pitch a VM. Going public is decided after the native launch (#687). **The `main` ruleset is the flow (#681, 2026-10-21):** every change, laptop and cloud, goes through a PR opened by the Builder bot; tier 1 and 2 merge on green CI plus the Reviewer bot's approval, tier 3 on the owner's own GitHub approval (auto-merge); no bypass actors, break-glass is a written procedure (`docs/architecture/commit-policy.md` > Break-glass). On a red e2e gate, debug and fix with bounded retries; don't revert on the first failure. Details: `docs/architecture/commit-policy.md`.
- **Three written risk tiers; owner approval on tier 3 only** (2026-10-20, #680). Every PR gets a `/review-pr` review before it merges. Tier 1 (everything else) gets a lighter, cheaper review; tier 2 (a weakened test, effort 5 or more) the full adversarial one; tier 3 (guardrails: `.github/`, `.claude/`, the gate and dispatch scripts, CODEOWNERS, this file, the commit policy; SQLite schema and migrations, native config and entitlements, any new network call; the old backend SQL and auth paths went with the PWA, #940) also waits for the owner. The tier 3 paths live in one file, `scripts/risk-tiers.json`, that CODEOWNERS is generated from. Details: `docs/backlog-runner.md` > Review.
- **Native-only checks go to the Android QA batch** (2026-10-12, #341). Trust e2e for app logic. Only behavior a real device can show gets a manual check, and it goes in `scripts/qa/procedure.mjs` plus the one open issue labeled `android-qa-batch`. Don't file per-ship test issues or ask for manual PWA testing. iPhone-only checks (anything in `ios/`, or behavior only an iPhone shows) go the same way into the one open issue labeled `iphone-qa-batch` (#33, 2026-10-21): a line each, checked once on a real iPhone, leaving out anything Android, the PWA, or e2e already covers. See `docs/qa-procedure.md`.
- **"New Android build" means the Play internal track** (2026-10-11): `release-play.yml` with `track=internal`. The personal flavor only when the owner says "personal", "dev", or "Obtainium". Don't use Cut release unless a versioned store release is asked for, because it also tags the commit and starts iOS.
- **Lanes run on their own under an Actions minutes guard; the owner works their queue from a live session** (2026-11-02, #904; replaces the 2026-10-22 pause). On 2026-10-22 the owner disabled Dispatch backlog lanes, Dispatch support triage, Dispatch owner calendar, and Human issue digest to stay inside Pro's 3,000 Actions minutes a month, because the owner can't pay overage or make the repo public yet (#687). Measured afterward: October used 2,429 minutes on the billing page. The busy stretches ran at 3,000 to 3,900 a month, first from the dispatcher itself (fixed 2026-10-15), then from CI on each PR and from releases (about 30 minutes each); the four paused workflows cost about 20 a day. So the pause was reasonable but blunt. The working rule now: those workflows are back on, `LANE_RUNS_PER_DAY` is 10, and the minutes guard in `scripts/run-budget.mjs` halves that cap once the month is ahead of an even pace and stops lanes at 90% of the allowance, keeping the rest for CI and a release (`docs/backlog-runner.md` > Run budget). Releases are about weekly (each costs about 30 minutes, mostly the e2e gate). Anything only the owner can decide waits on GitHub, and the owner clears it from a live session with `/waiting`, whose list the session start hook counts (`docs/backlog-runner.md` > Owner queue). PRs still ship through the Builder bot, and Review (Opus) still reviews each one. Don't pitch more Actions usage (per-PR e2e, screenshot tests, new scheduled workflows) without saying what it costs a month against the guard. UI regressions are caught by the release smoke pass (#803).
- **No local loop runner.** The old backlog loop script and its skill were removed on purpose (2026-10-15, #587): they cost far more of the plan than separate `/next-item` sessions, and the lane routines (#236) plus their dispatcher replace them. Don't rebuild one.
- **Anything the owner must do on or after a date goes on their Google Calendar** (2026-10-21). A dated ask, a "check back on" step, a deadline, or an `On hold until` date gets an event titled `RepBunny: <what> (#N)` with the steps and the issue link in the description, and no duplicates (search by title first; move it if the date changes). An interactive session with the Google Calendar connector adds it right away; headless runs mark the issue so it gets synced (#746). Agent-only dates (a lane checking something itself) don't need an event: the lane snoozes the issue with a not-before date (at most 30 days) so the picker skips it until then, never `status:blocked` plus an owner step (#749). Lanes get no access to the owner's calendar.
- **Always offer the top `human` issue** in an interactive `/next-item`, even if its latest comment says it's stalled. Mention the stall reason so the owner can skip it themselves.
- **Run the e2e gate with `PW_WORKERS=2`** until the owner says the laptop has more RAM (2026-10-09; a 4-worker run starved it). When memory is short, stopping stale Vite dev servers and Chrome is allowed without asking, then rerun the suite in the foreground.
- **Verify UI changes visually.** If the Chrome extension isn't connected, use a throwaway Playwright screenshot spec at 390x844 (delete it afterward) instead of skipping the check.
- **Shared worktree.** The runner may edit files in the same folder as an interactive session. Run `git status` before committing, `git add` only your own files (never `-A`), and prefer filing issues over editing repo files mid-run.
- **Commit messages:** write `git commit -F` files with the Write tool or a bash heredoc, never PowerShell 5.1 `Out-File -Encoding utf8`, which puts a BOM in the subject (it shipped once, and fixing it needs a force-push the repo forbids).
- **Swipe and drag gestures:** `setPointerCapture` on a container silently breaks clicks on buttons and links inside it. For touch swipes use `touchstart` and `touchend` from any target with no capture, keep pointer events for mouse only, and test them with CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent` in e2e (dc84f7f).
- **Code is shaped for the agents that read it** (2026-10-16). Agents re-read what they open on every later turn, so file size and test feedback matter more than for human readers. Three rules:
  - **File-size ratchet:** no file under `src/` grows past 800 lines. Files already over it are on a baseline list and can't grow; new code for one goes in a new component or composable beside it. The list only shrinks. `WorkoutLogger.vue` (5,975 lines) is the main case. Enforcement: #626.
  - **No abstraction without a second implementation:** don't add an interface, factory, or injection layer unless two real implementations exist now. `Repos` qualified when the PWA's backend adapter sat beside SQLite and stays as the data boundary. An extra hop is cheap with prompt caching; the cost is a reader who has to learn a layer that buys nothing.
  - **Bug fixes land with a regression spec that failed before the fix**, and the close-out says so. Enforcement: #627.
  - **No refactoring just to make agents cheaper** (2026-10-16, #628). Splitting `WorkoutLogger.vue` made small changes about 17% cheaper and no more correct, a saving of about $0.10 per change, so the split wasn't shipped. Don't propose or file a structural refactor on cost grounds unless `/process-review`'s cost-per-change data shows a hot spot that costs clearly more than the rest.
  Cost per change is recorded in every `next-item` close-out (`scripts/run-cost.mjs`) and compared monthly by `/process-review`, so these rules can be judged by whether same-size changes got cheaper. Experiment: #628.
- **Scoring is the filer's job.** Whoever files an issue sets `effort:N` and `value:N` themselves and never asks the owner for the numbers. See `docs/backlog.md`.

## Writing and public communication

- **Outbound text sounds human.** Anything sent under the owner's name or the product's (emails, support replies, GitHub comments others read, release notes, store or site copy, posts) goes through the `humanizer` skill first. No em dashes.
- **Credit the owner's role.** In public writing, the owner designs the system that lets agents work without lowering quality, makes the decisions, and tests every release. Never "AI built it" or "I'm not writing it myself". Line for non-developers: "I make the decisions, they do the typing, and nothing goes out without my testing it."
- **Public posts describe RepBunny as iPhone and Android only.** The retired PWA and its backend were private to the pilot and aren't mentioned. Frame the automation as safe because the shipped app keeps all data on the phone.
- **Testers aren't named in the repo** (#223, #942). The owner's messages may name a designated tester (speech-to-text can misspell the name; issues in this private repo keep the spelling #223 settled). Files, commits, and PR text say "the iPhone tester" instead (`docs/public-content.md`).
- **Voice of RepBunny:** ElevenLabs "Jessica" (voice ID `cgSgspJ2msm6clMCkdW9`) narrates guide clips and previews (#424, setup #425). Only our own script text goes to ElevenLabs, never user data.
- **The owner's own voice** (2026-10-16): an ElevenLabs clone of the owner, "Deaner2" (voice ID `X8kRkdXqxXKyw5xP1oP6`), narrates release demos (the sprint review videos, #647) and other promotional videos where the owner speaks as themselves. Jessica stays the in-app voice (guide clips, lifting basics, previews).
- **Model: `eleven_v4` with audio tags** (2026-10-16, owner's pick after the #643 listening test). Tags like `[warm, friendly]`, `[excited]`, `[chuckles]` direct the delivery. v4 keeps `previous_text` / `next_text` (v3 rejects them) and bills the same per character as v2; the tags count as characters. Moving the pipeline over is #648.
- **Write spoken text for text-to-speech** (2026-10-15). Release notes (read aloud in the sprint review, #606), clip and guide narration lines, and anything else voiced use short, plain sentences with no colons, semicolons, dashes, or parentheses, and spell out symbols and abbreviations ("10 to 20 sets", "pounds", "one rep max"). They make the voice hesitate. Audio tags in square brackets are the one exception: they direct the voice and aren't read aloud. No further model hunting; v4 is settled. Full rule: `scripts/demo-video/review/README.md` > Writing for the voice.
